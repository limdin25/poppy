An unsold lot is the one place at an auction where you can buy without bidding against the room. The seller set a reserve, nobody reached it, and now the auctioneer has to find a buyer. They want this call. Your job is five things: is it still there, what will the seller take, is it empty, how do they sell it now, and can we see it.
NEVER agree a price and never say "we'll take it". After an auction the sale goes through on the auction's own terms: you exchange on the spot, 10% deposit, no survey, no backing out. A yes on the phone can be treated as a yes. Every figure goes to the director first. Read the blue lines aloud; grey lines are for you.
More than one lot with this office? Ask about all of them on this one call. Run the five questions for the first lot, then "and while I have you, lot 14 as well" with the next lot's number. Pick each lot in the Lots tab as you go so the screen matches what you are asking.
1. Is it still available
YOU: "Hi, it's Pedro from Unico. I'm calling about lot [lot_number], [lot_address]. It was in your sale on [auction_date] and I can see it didn't sell. Is it still available?"
Then stop and let them answer.
✓ "Yes, it's still available", keep reading ↓
"It's sold" / "it's under offer" obj
YOU: "Ah, fair enough. Has it exchanged yet? If it falls through, would you give me a call?"
Get their name. Mark the lot Sold or under offer and move on to the next lot with this office.
"Which lot?" obj
YOU: "Lot [lot_number], [lot_address], guide was [guide_price]."
Read it off the Lots tab. They look lots up by number.
You get a switchboard obj
Ask for "post-auction sales" or "the team handling the unsold lots". If you reach voicemail, hang up and mark Voicemail, it comes round again tomorrow.
2. What will the seller take
YOU: "What would the seller accept for it now? Have you had any offers since the sale?"
Write down the exact figure they say, and any offer they mention. This is the number the director decides on. Do not react to it, good or bad.
✓ They give a figure, write it in the Lots tab, keep reading ↓
"What's your offer?" obj
YOU: "I'm not the one who sets the figure, that's my director. What would the seller need to get it done? I'll put it to him today."
Never say a number of ours. Not the guide, not a round figure, nothing.
"The reserve is confidential" obj
YOU: "Understood. Roughly where would an offer need to be for you to take it to the seller?"
Most will give you a range. Write it down.
"It's best and final bids by a date" obj
YOU: "No problem. What's the deadline, and how do bids go in, by email?"
Write the date and the email down. That deadline goes straight to the director.
3. Is it empty, and anything in the pack
YOU: "Is it vacant, or is somebody living there?"
YOU: "Anything in the legal pack or the condition I should know about? Roof, damp, anything structural?"
Tenanted is usually a no for us: say thanks, mark Not suitable with "tenanted" in the note. A leak or damp is not a no, it is a reason the price comes down. Write it down.
4. How they sell it now
YOU: "If the director's happy, how does it work from here? Is it the auction contract, 10% on exchange, and what's the completion date and the buyer's fee?"
You are only collecting the terms. Write them down.
5. See it, and get the pack
YOU: "Can we get in to see it? And could you email me the legal pack?"
YOU: "Great. I'll take it to my director Hugo and come back to you today."
A viewing only matters for a lot that is still available, empty, and at a figure the director says yes to, so take the day they offer and write it in the note. Our builder goes, not you. Get the email address the pack comes from, and the name of the person you spoke to.
