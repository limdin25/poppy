Ringing the agent about [property_address]

You are calling on behalf of the director, who is a cash buyer. Your job is to find out about the house and gauge how flexible the vendor is. You do not make a formal offer and you do not book a viewing. Read the blue lines aloud. Grey lines are notes for you only. One question at a time, and react to what they say before asking the next thing. If they push back, open the amber button on that line.
1. Is it still available
YOU: "Hi, hello. I'm calling about the property on [property_street], the [bedrooms] bed [property_type]. Is that one still available?"
Then stop. Nothing else. This one question tells you whether there is a call to have at all, and asking it plainly makes you sound like a buyer rather than a salesman.

YOU: "Ah, fair enough. Would the vendor consider backup offers at all?"
Worth 20 seconds. Chains fall through constantly and a backup offer costs nothing. Note what they say, thank them, wrap up. Mark the outcome Not qualified unless they say yes to a backup, in which case mark it Callback.

2. Ask for the two minutes
YOU: "Oh lovely. So, I'm calling on behalf of our director, he's a cash buyer. Mind if I ask a couple of quick questions? Then he can call you back himself."
Wait for the yes. "Cash buyer" is the reason they will give you two minutes, so do not bury it. "He'll call you back himself" tells them this call is not the pitch, which takes the pressure off both of you.
✓ "Yeah, go on", keep reading ↓

YOU: "Course. When's good, later today or tomorrow morning?"
Take the time, mark Callback, move on. Do not try to squeeze it in.
They sound rushed but will talk obj
Drop to the four that matter: occupancy, condition, why they're selling, then go straight to the money. Skip the rest.
3. The checklist
Conversational, not a form. Acknowledge each answer, "ah okay", "that makes sense", "lovely", before the next question. If they volunteer something, do not ask it again. Tick them off in the Houses tab on the right as you go.
YOU: "Is it vacant, or is there a tenant in?"
If tenanted: are they staying or leaving, and what rent are they paying?
YOU: "And what sort of condition is it in, ready to move into or does it need work?"
Then the big ones: roof, damp, electrics, boiler. This is what moves the number.
YOU: "Has it had much interest? Any offers so far?"
And: has a sale ever fallen through on it? A fall-through is the single best sign a vendor will take less.
YOU: "Do you know why they're selling?"
Then: are they in a hurry, and is there an onward chain? Motivation is worth more than any other answer on this page.
YOU: "Is it freehold or leasehold?"
Their answer decides which column below you use. Ask the wrong set and you sound like you have never bought a house.

If it is a flat or maisonette

  • Years left on the lease
  • Service charge per year
  • Ground rent
  • Any big one-off bills or major works planned for the block
  • Taller block? Any cladding or EWS1 issues

If it is a house or bungalow

  • Confirm it is freehold
  • Structural: roof, damp, subsidence
  • Any extensions or conversions done, and were they signed off

Never

  • Never ask a house about service charges or a lease, unless they say it is leasehold
  • Never ask a flat about subsidence, unless they raise it

Asking them for the number is the whole trick. Any figure they say is worth more than any figure you say. Write it down in the Houses tab exactly as they said it.

5. Wrap up
YOU: "And what do viewings look like, weekdays, weekends, how much notice?"
Ask, but do not book one. The director arranges viewings himself. Offering to book is the fastest way to be asked a question you cannot answer.
YOU: "That's great, thanks for your time. The director will be in touch."
Then wait. Do not hang up on your own closing line. They often add the most useful thing in the call after they think it is over.
BEFORE YOU MOVE ON
Tick off what you learned in the Houses tab and pick an outcome. If they gave you a figure, type it in. That number is the reason this call happened.
Never invent anything about the property, the director or the financing. You only know what is on this page. If they ask for company details beyond the name, the director will share them when he calls back.
